## The question

When a language model answers a logic puzzle correctly, two very different
things might have happened. It might have derived the answer. Or it might have
seen the puzzle before.

Standard benchmarks cannot tell these apart, because they score only the final
answer. MMLU, BIG-Bench Hard and ARC all reward the right output regardless of
how it was reached, which means a model can score well on surface recall alone.
Nothing in them asks whether the *same* problem, restated so that its logical
structure is untouched but its wording is unrecognisable, still gets solved.

That gap is narrow but consequential. Prior benchmarks that vary their problems
— GSM-Symbolic, for instance — swap entities and values while leaving the
reasoning template fixed. They test generalisation across instantiations of one
logical form. The question here is the inverse: hold the truth conditions
constant and vary the logical form itself.

## The approach

Logifus is an obfuscation function that maps a question to a logically
equivalent counterpart. Because the two versions are equivalent, they share a
correct answer by construction — so any drop in accuracy is a fact about the
model, not about the problem.

Each of four task types gets its own transformation. First-order logic premises
are rewritten through chained equivalences — De Morgan's laws, contraposition,
double-negation elimination — so that "if it rains, the ground gets wet" becomes
"the ground does not stay dry whenever it rains." Kinship statements are
replaced by indirect multi-hop descriptions, turning "A is the brother of B"
into "A is the sister-in-law's husband of B," and at the harder level into
chains four relations deep. Numeric sequences are re-encoded through a
deterministic pipeline: digits become planet names, planet names become ASCII
sums, ASCII sums become MD5 hashes. Navigation problems are padded with
self-cancelling detours whose vectors sum to zero, leaving net displacement
unchanged.

Equivalence is verified rather than assumed. For the first-order logic split,
the Prover9 theorem prover checks entailment formally before any human sees the
item. Every question in every split is then validated by two independent
annotators, with Cohen's κ between 0.88 and 0.95 depending on the task.

The result is LogiQAte: 1,108 questions across the four splits — 272 logic, 300
kinship, 284 numeric, 252 navigation. Ten models were evaluated in total,
across zero-shot, few-shot and chain-of-thought prompting at temperature 0.0,
scored by exact match after normalisation.

## What was found

Averaged across four tasks and three prompting settings, six frontier models
lose **28.8%** accuracy when a problem is restated equivalently. Zero-shot, the
per-model picture is worse: GPT-4o falls 47.6%, GPT-5 27.0%, o4-mini 21.4%.

The single most useful number is not a model's. Two undergraduate annotators
scored up to **99%** on both the original and the rewritten questions. The
rewrites are not hard. They are only hard for models — which is what separates
a genuine robustness failure from a benchmark that is simply difficult.

Reasoning-tuned models degrade less than general-purpose ones — roughly 20–30%
against 45–50% — but none is immune, and chain-of-thought helps unevenly.
Numeric obfuscation is the most destructive: under symbolic substitution,
several models collapse to near-zero.

Two mechanistic analyses on LLaMA 3.1 8B suggest why. A Min-K%++ membership
test finds that obfuscated kinship questions are *more* detectable as memorised
content than their originals, AUROC rising from 55–60% to 82% at the harder
obfuscation level — consistent with models leaning harder on recall exactly
when the surface form stops matching what they recall. And layer-wise
next-token confidence in layers 28–31, where semantic consolidation is expected
to happen, is suppressed by 50–75% on kinship items and over 80% on logic
items.

Manual inspection of GPT-5's traces shows the failures are usually localised
rather than total. On one numeric item the model decoded the MD5 hashes
correctly, identified the underlying pattern correctly, computed the right
intermediate value — then continued the sequence past the point it needed and
reported the wrong term.

## Limitations

The benchmark is English-only, and covers four reasoning categories. Extending
it to other languages, particularly low-resource ones, and to categories such
as seating arrangements and commonsense inference remains open, as does moving
from fixed transformations to adaptive ones that respond to a model's
particular weaknesses.

The mechanistic results carry a narrower scope than the accuracy results. Both
the memorization test and the layer-wise analysis ran on a single open model,
LLaMA 3.1 8B, chosen because the analyses require access to internal
activations that the frontier APIs do not expose. They are a plausible
mechanism for the degradation measured elsewhere, not a demonstration that the
same mechanism operates inside GPT-5 or Claude.

Obfuscation difficulty is also not controlled independently of surface form. A
rewrite that is longer, or that stacks four transformations rather than one, is
harder in more than one way at once, and the design does not separate those
factors.
