## The question

GPTQ, OmniQuant, and ResQ allocate bit-width across layers using the
activation-covariance Hessian trace as a per-layer sensitivity signal: layers
with larger activation norms are judged more sensitive and are given more
bits. The premise is that this proxy tracks which layers matter functionally.
None of the three methods tests that premise against task performance —
only against reconstruction error.

## The approach

Quantization is treated as a controlled perturbation probe, the way a focal
lesion is used in neuroscience to identify which region supports which
function. The model is fully NF4-quantized, then FP16 weights are surgically
restored to one sub-layer at a time — one of 32 attention projections or 32
MLP blocks — and task accuracy is measured after each restoration. Recovery
is conditioned on forward-flipped examples: the subset of a benchmark correct
under FP16 but wrong under 4-bit. Conditioning on the flip set is what makes
recovery rates comparable across GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, and MATH despite their
different absolute accuracies.

## What was found

Restoration rank across all 64 sub-layers correlates with Hessian
sensitivity rank at Spearman ρ = −0.506 (p < 0.001, n = 64): inverted, not
merely absent. Under Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across 192 tests, the
inversion resolves into a clean crossover — no attention layer survives for
ARC, no MLP layer survives for GSM8K. Attention layers 13 and 14 are
task-selective: 52.1% GSM8K recovery, under 15% on ARC and MATH. MLP layers 6
and 7 are shared across tasks instead, recovering 25–48% on all three. The
pattern replicates on Mistral-7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill, and holds when the
quantization method is switched from NF4 to GPTQ. A surgical model that keeps
just 6 of 32 layers at full precision reaches statistical equivalence with
FP16 while using 42% of its memory. Weight-space SQNR, checked as a negative
control, is uncorrelated with functional importance (ρ = +0.025, not
significant) — the inversion is specific to the Hessian trace, not an
artifact of measuring quantization error in general.

## Limitations

The study covers only instruction-tuned, Pre-LN/RMSNorm transformers.
Base-model replication was attempted on OPT-6.7B and Pythia-6.9B, but
zero-shot ARC scored below chance on both (22% and 19%), which rules out
accuracy-based restoration ranking for non-instruction-tuned models. The
recovery metric is bounded by small flip-sets — 31 examples for GSM8K, 36 for
ARC, 24 for MATH — which leaves MATH underpowered rather than establishing
that it is precision-insensitive. The HESS-6 baseline used for the
head-to-head comparison ranks layers by activation-covariance trace alone;
it isolates the bit-allocation signal that GPTQ, OmniQuant, and ResQ share,
not a full production allocator, which in deployed systems also folds in
outlier protection and kernel-fused dequantization. Bootstrap confidence
intervals on ρ and per-task recovery are deferred to camera-ready.
